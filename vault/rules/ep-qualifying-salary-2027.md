# What changes

Two hurdles, and the salary is only the first.

**Stage 1 — qualifying salary.** Rises progressively with age from 23:

| | Now (entry, age 23 and below) | Rises with age to (45+) |
|---|---|---|
| All sectors except financial services | S$5,600 | S$10,700 |
| **From 1 Jan 2027** | **S$6,000** | **S$11,500** |
| Financial services | S$6,200 | S$11,800 |
| **Financial services from 1 Jan 2027** | **S$6,600** | **S$12,700** |

**Stage 2 — COMPASS.** The Complementarity Assessment Framework scores candidates across
six criteria including salary benchmarking, qualifications, workforce diversity, local
employment support, skills shortages, and strategic economic priorities. An applicant must
score **at least 40 points**.

**Meeting the salary floor is not sufficient.** A candidate can clear stage 1 and still
fail COMPASS.

# Who it hits

Every EP holder and every EP application. The age scaling is severe — at 45+ the
requirement is nearly double the entry figure — so a founder or senior hire in their
forties faces a far higher bar than the headline number suggests.

# Cost

The gap between current salary and the applicable age-banded floor, times 12. An EP holder
at S$5,800 against a S$6,000 entry floor is S$2,400 a year. A 40-something on a package set
years ago can be tens of thousands short.

A failed renewal means **losing the person**, which for a startup usually means losing the
ability to deliver whatever they were building.

# Next step

For each EP holder, look up their exact age-banded requirement on the MOM page — do not
use the entry figure. Then check the COMPASS score separately, because salary alone does
not decide it. Where a renewal is at risk, start restructuring the package or the role at
least two quarters ahead.

Confirm the exact renewal cut-off date for EP on the MOM page before relying on a timeline;
this note verifies the salary tables and the COMPASS threshold, not the renewal cut-off.
