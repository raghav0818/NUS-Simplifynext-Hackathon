# What changes

Nothing changes in the law. **The date arrives.** A private (non-listed) company must file
its annual return with ACRA **within 7 months after its financial year end**.

Worked example from ACRA: FYE 31 December 2025 gives a filing deadline of **31 July 2026**.

# Who it hits

Every private company, every year, on a date derived from its own FYE — which is why no
two companies share a deadline and why a generic calendar reminder does not help.

# Cost

Two-tier penalty framework, for annual lodgments due on or after 14 January 2022:

| Filed | Penalty |
|---|---|
| Within 3 months after the due date | **S$300** |
| More than 3 months after the due date | **S$600** |

ACRA's example: deadline 31 July 2026, filed 20 August 2026 (20 days late) — S$300.

Beyond the fee, persistent late filing is an enforcement matter for the directors
personally, not just the company.

# Next step

Take your FYE, add 7 months, and put that date in the calendar with a 6-week warning.
If a corporate secretary files on your behalf, confirm in writing who owns the deadline —
the penalty and the director liability stay with you regardless of who was supposed to file.
