# What changes

Nothing about the law changes. **You cross a line.** Businesses must monitor their own
sales and register once annual taxable turnover exceeds **S$1 million**. Two separate
tests, and either one triggers the obligation:

**Retrospective view** — taxable turnover for the calendar year (1 Jan to 31 Dec)
exceeded S$1 million. Apply between **1 Jan and 30 Jan of the following year**.
Registration takes effect **1 Mar**.

**Prospective view** — at any point, if you can reasonably expect taxable turnover to
exceed S$1 million in the next 12 months. Apply **within 30 days of the date of your
forecast**. Registration takes effect on the 31st day after the forecast date.

Where liability arises on a prospective basis on or after 1 Jul 2025, there is a
two-month grace period before you must start charging GST — but the 30-day application
deadline still stands.

# Who it hits

Any growing company approaching S$1 million in taxable turnover. The prospective test is
the one founders miss: the obligation begins when you can **reasonably forecast**
crossing, not when you actually cross.

# Cost

Late notification: a fine of **up to S$10,000** plus a penalty equal to **10% of the GST
due**. Fines and penalties are generally waived if you voluntarily disclose the late
notification when you submit the registration.

Beyond the penalty, registration means charging 9% GST on sales — repricing decisions,
contract amendments, and invoicing changes that take weeks to work through.

# Next step

Check your rolling 12-month taxable turnover today. If a reasonable forecast puts you
above S$1 million within 12 months, the 30-day clock has already started — apply now.
If you are approaching but not yet forecasting a crossing, set a review date and decide
in advance whether you absorb the 9% or pass it to customers.
